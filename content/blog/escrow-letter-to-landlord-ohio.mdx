*This article is general information about Ohio law, not legal advice. Procedures and forms vary by court, so confirm the details with your local clerk of courts or a qualified attorney.*

## What is an escrow letter to a landlord in Ohio?

It is the written notice a tenant gives a landlord about a problem with the rental before depositing rent with a court. Ohio's escrow remedy comes from [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07), which lets a tenant pay rent to the clerk of court rather than to the landlord when the landlord fails to fix a covered problem after notice. The notice, and the landlord's failure to fix the problem afterward, is what opens that remedy.

Without proper notice, the escrow can backfire. Under [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321), a landlord may ask the court to release escrowed rent if the tenant did not comply with the notice requirement or was not current on rent when the deposits began. If the court finds either, it must order the rent released to the landlord, less costs.

## When can an Ohio tenant use rent escrow?

When the landlord fails to meet a duty under R.C. 5321.04 or a duty in the rental agreement. [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07) says remedies also arise when conditions lead the tenant to reasonably believe the landlord has failed, or when a governmental agency has found a failure. The one exception is the drug-activity duty in R.C. 5321.04(A)(9).

The landlord's baseline duties are in [R.C. 5321.04](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.04). They include complying with building, housing, health and safety codes that materially affect health and safety, and making repairs and doing whatever is reasonably necessary to keep the premises fit and habitable.

## How long must I wait after sending the letter?

The landlord gets a reasonable time given how severe the problem is, or thirty days, whichever is sooner. Under [R.C. 5321.07(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07), if the landlord has not fixed the condition by then, and the tenant is current on rent, the tenant may choose a remedy.

So a serious problem, such as no heat in winter, may justify a shorter wait than thirty days, but thirty days is the outer limit. Being current on rent matters: under [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321), a landlord can seek release of escrowed funds if the tenant was behind when deposits began.

## What are my options once the waiting period passes?

You have three, per [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07):

1. Deposit all rent due and thereafter due with the clerk of the municipal or county court.
2. Apply to the court for an order directing the landlord to remedy the condition.
3. Terminate the rental agreement.

If you apply for a court order under option two, the statute also lets you deposit rent, ask for an order reducing the periodic rent until the landlord fixes the problem, and ask for an order allowing the deposited rent to be used to remedy the condition.

Escrow is not the only route. A party's filing in the Supreme Court of Ohio's [case 2021-1310](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/pdf_viewer/pdf_viewer.aspx?pdf=912049.pdf&subdirectory=2021-1310%5CDocketItems) makes the same point: the statute gives a right to escrow rent, but escrow is not the only remedy. That is a party's argument, not a ruling.

## What happens after I deposit rent with the clerk?

The clerk must give written notice of the deposit to the landlord and to the landlord's agent, if there is one, under [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321). The court then controls the funds. A recent appellate opinion, [Bloor v. Barnes](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10849123/bloor-v-barnes/), addresses a § 5321.07 escrow hearing, the notice requirement, and the court's authority over escrowed funds.

Court fees, forms, and deposit steps differ by court, so ask your local clerk before you pay.

## What are the risks of escrowing rent?

A tenant who deposits rent can be held liable if the court finds the condition resulted from the tenant's own act or omission, or if the tenant intentionally acted in bad faith. [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321) says liability covers the landlord's damages and costs, and reasonable attorney's fees are added when the bad faith was intentional.

In short: escrow only for conditions the landlord is responsible for, keep rent current before you start, and keep a dated copy of your notice and any proof of delivery.

## Can my landlord retaliate for my complaint?

Not in the ways the statute lists. Under [R.C. 5321.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.02), a landlord may not retaliate by raising rent, decreasing services, or bringing or threatening an eviction action because the tenant complained to a government agency about a code violation, complained to the landlord about a violation of R.C. 5321.04, or joined other tenants to negotiate collectively.

If a landlord retaliates, the tenant may use the retaliation as a defense to a possession action, or recover possession, and may recover actual damages. The text described here comes from the [1974 authenticated version of R.C. 5321.02(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/assets/laws/revised-code/authenticated/53/5321/5321.02/11-4-1974/5321.02-11-4-1974.pdf), so check the current statute for any later changes.

## How do I put the notice in writing?

Give the notice in writing, keep a dated copy, and keep proof of how and when you delivered it. Before sending, read [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07) itself to confirm what the notice must contain and how it must be delivered, since those details should be checked against the current statute rather than assumed. ## FAQ

**Do I have to send my landlord a letter before escrowing rent in Ohio?**
The escrow remedy under [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07) is triggered when the landlord has been given notice and fails to fix the problem in time. A deposit made without complying with the notice requirement can lead to release of the funds to the landlord, per [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321), so put your notice in writing.

**How long must I wait after giving notice?**
A reasonable time given the severity of the condition, or thirty days, whichever is sooner, and you must be current on rent under [R.C. 5321.07(B)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07).

**Where does the escrowed rent go?**
To the clerk of the municipal or county court, which notifies the landlord in writing, per [Chapter 5321](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321).

**Can my landlord retaliate if I complain?**
Not by raising rent, decreasing services, or bringing or threatening eviction for a protected complaint under [R.C. 5321.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.02).

**Is escrow my only remedy?**
No. You can also ask the court to order repairs or terminate the rental agreement under [R.C. 5321.07](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5321.07).
