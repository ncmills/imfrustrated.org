*This article is general information, not legal advice. Procedures and deadlines are set locally and can change, so confirm them with the agency named on your notice.*

## What is the Bureau of Administrative Adjudication?

The name does not point to one confirmed national agency. In New York, [the DMV's Traffic Violations Bureau (TVB) adjudicates non-criminal traffic tickets in the five boroughs](https://dmv.ny.gov/tickets/find-tvb-office-locations), and [a DMV form carries the label "Office of Administrative Adjudication/Traffic Violations Bureau"](https://dmv.ny.gov/forms/aa532.pdf). This article could not confirm a current official body named exactly "Bureau of Administrative Adjudication," so treat the phrase as a general label for the office that hears your ticket.

## Where does administrative adjudication come from?

It comes from state law that lets local governments hear minor violations outside criminal court. In Illinois, 625 ILCS 5/11-208.3 allows a municipality or county to create, by ordinance, an administrative system for standing and parking violations, vehicle compliance violations, automated traffic law violations, and automated speed enforcement violations. The Illinois General Assembly's statute page could not be verified as live when this article was prepared, so look up the current text of 625 ILCS 5/11-208.3 on ilga.gov. The City of Chicago's own [Department of Administrative Hearings vehicle FAQs](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/faq/vehicle_faqs.html) describe how the city handles these cases in practice.

## Who handles these tickets in Chicago?

In Chicago, the [Department of Administrative Hearings](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/faq/vehicle_faqs.html) handles vehicle hearings. A Chicago Consolidated Notice of Violation [gives 21 days to pay or contest, and hearings can be by correspondence, virtual, or in person](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html). Check your notice for the hearing options available for your ticket.

## What happens if I ignore a ticket?

A late-payment penalty follows. Chicago's Department of Finance says [the penalty is the lesser of the original fine or $250 minus the original fine](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html). If a default determination is entered, you can petition to set it aside by appearing within 21 days of the initial default determination date. [If you miss that window, you lose the option to contest the ticket administratively or judicially](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html).

## How long do I have to pay or appeal after a decision?

In Chicago, a person found liable has [25 days from the date of decision to pay before the ticket doubles, and 35 days from the decision date to appeal](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/faq/vehicle_faqs.html). The appeal goes to the Circuit Court of Cook County (Daley Center, 50 W. Washington, Room 602), as described in the same [Vehicle FAQs](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/faq/vehicle_faqs.html).

## How do I appeal a hearing officer's decision?

You appeal to court, not back to the hearing office. [Either side may appeal to the Circuit Court of Cook County within 35 days by filing a civil lawsuit for administrative review and paying the applicable state filing fee](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/the_hearing_process/appealing_a_decision.html). This article did not verify the current filing fee, so confirm it with the court clerk.

## What if my license is suspended over parking tickets?

Act fast. In Chicago, a license suspension for parking tickets [must be contested in writing within 21 days of the Secretary of State's notice](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/faq/vehicle_faqs.html), mailed to the Dept. of Administrative Hearings, Vehicle Hearings Division/DLS, 740 N. Sedgwick St., 2nd Floor.

## How do I put my contest in writing?

A clear, dated letter that states the ticket number, the facts, and what you ask the hearing office to decide helps keep your dispute organized. Follow the contest instructions on your notice; for a Chicago ticket, the [Consolidated Notice](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html) explains the 21-day window to pay or contest. ## FAQ

### Is there one official Bureau of Administrative Adjudication?

This article could not confirm a current official body with exactly that name. The closest match found is the New York DMV's "Office of Administrative Adjudication/Traffic Violations Bureau" label on [a DMV form](https://dmv.ny.gov/forms/aa532.pdf). In Chicago, the equivalent work is done by the Department of Administrative Hearings.

### How long do I have to appeal a Chicago hearing decision?

35 days from the decision, by filing for administrative review in the Circuit Court of Cook County, per the [City's appeal guidance](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ah/supp_info/the_hearing_process/appealing_a_decision.html).

### Can I contest a Chicago ticket without going in person?

The [Consolidated Notice](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html) says hearings can be by correspondence, virtual, or in person.

### What if I miss the deadline after a default finding?

You have 21 days from the initial default determination date to petition to set it aside; after that you lose the option to contest administratively or judicially, according to the [City's Consolidated Notice page](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/revenue/parking_and_red-lightnoticeinformation5/violation_status.html).

### Does the same timeline apply in New York?

Not necessarily. This article did not confirm New York hearing deadlines or appeal procedures, so check your ticket and the [NY DMV](https://dmv.ny.gov/tickets/find-tvb-office-locations).
